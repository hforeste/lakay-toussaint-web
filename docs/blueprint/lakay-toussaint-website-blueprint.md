PAGE 2 — OUR STORY (Mission and History)

MISYON NOU × OUR MISSION

Mission statement (large display text): > Lakay Toussaint Community Alliance is a nonprofit organization established to celebrate, preserve, and advance the culture, heritage, and wellbeing of the Haitian community in Seattle and the Pacific Northwest.

Tagline: No Haitian Left Behind.

Founding story

Lakay Toussaint was born the way the best Haitian things are — around food, music, and family. What began as a community picnic in 2025 revealed something undeniable: Haitians in the Pacific Northwest were hungry for home. Founded by the community and named by Abby Joseph, one of its founders, Lakay Toussaint Community Alliance exists to make that home permanent — a lakay for Haitian culture and community in Seattle and beyond.

Who we are (three short paragraphs)

Our name carries our purpose. Lakay means home in Haitian Creole, and Toussaint Louverture led the revolution that made Haiti the first free Black republic in 1804 — a legacy every Haitian carries wherever we land, including here in Seattle.

More than 1.1 million Haitian Americans call the United States home, yet the overwhelming majority live along the East Coast. Here in the Pacific Northwest, we are a community of only a few thousand — an estimated 4,000 to 5,000 people of Haitian ancestry across Washington, Oregon, Alaska, and Idaho, growing every year, with none of the established Haitian institutions our brothers and sisters enjoy in Miami, New York, or Boston. That is exactly why Lakay Toussaint exists.

From 1804: A Haitian Independence Day Celebration — the first of its kind in Seattle’s history — to Jou Drapo Ayisyen, our Haitian Flag Day celebration every May 18, to A Taste of Haiti, our annual Labor Day celebration of food, music, and family, we bring the warmth of a Haitian lakay to the Pacific Northwest, and give the diaspora a place to gather, celebrate, and belong. Because no Haitian should ever feel far from home, and no Haitian will be left behind.

(Data source for grants: U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 5-Year Estimates, Table B04006 — approx. 3,100 in WA, 1,100 in OR, 700 in AK, 400 in ID. Survey data undercounts newer immigrant communities; “4,000 to 5,000 and growing” is a defensible, conservative figure.)

Program 1 — FLAGSHIP (badge: “Priyorite ane sa a × Our focus this year”)

Pwogram Sipò Imigrasyon × Immigration Support Program

In this moment, nothing matters more. As Haitian families across the country face uncertainty over TPS and immigration status, Lakay Toussaint is making immigration support our top priority this year. We help community members navigate the journey with trusted information and connections to qualified legal help: TPS and status renewals, work authorization, family petitions, and know-your-rights workshops — alongside the essentials that newly arrived families need to stabilize, from housing navigation to healthcare enrollment and school support. We connect families directly to accredited immigration attorneys, nonprofit legal clinics, and service agencies across the region — in Kreyòl, with someone who understands, every step of the way. No one in our lakay walks into an immigration process alone.

Year-one goal: Walk alongside 60 families through immigration navigation and arrival support. Button: Get immigration help × Jwenn èd imigrasyon (Developer: add disclaimer — “Lakay Toussaint is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice; we connect community members with qualified legal service providers.”)

Program 2

Pwogram Jèn Lidè Demen × Youth Academic & Leadership Program

Our young people are not the future of the community — they are the pride of it right now. Through tutoring, leadership workshops, career panels, and college bootcamps covering everything from FAFSA to the SAT, we’re equipping 50 Haitian students from middle school through college to lead — in their classrooms, in their community, and beyond.

Button: Enroll a student × Enskri yon elèv

Program 3

Pwogram Endepandans Ekonomik × Workforce & Economic Mobility Program

Haitians built the first free Black republic — building a career is in our blood. In partnership with workforce development organizations across the Puget Sound, we move Haitian adults into living-wage employment through resume preparation, English classes, professional certification pathways, financial literacy, and small business coaching. Our year-one goal: 60 community members enrolled and placed into certifications and jobs.

Button: Start your path × Kòmanse chemen ou

The Fund (donation centerpiece)

Fon Ijans Fanmi × The Family Emergency Fund

In a lakay, when one family struggles, the whole house responds. The Family Emergency Fund provides rapid help with rent, utilities, food, and emergency travel when a Haitian family faces crisis — neighbor to neighbor, with dignity and without red tape. We’re building this fund to $50,000 so that no family in our community ever faces a hard moment alone. Every dollar given goes directly to a family in need.

Button: Give to the fund × Bay nan fon an

THE BRIDGE HOME (its own section — the fourth pillar)

Rebati Ayiti × Rebuilding Haiti

A lakay never forgets where it comes from. While we build community here in the Pacific Northwest, our hearts remain with home. Lakay Toussaint is building partnerships with trusted community leaders on the ground in Haiti to support rebuilding efforts — done the right way: powering people who have been doing the work, with the community, all along. This work is coming soon. Lakay isit, lakay lòtbò — home here, home over there.

PATNÈ NOU YO × HOW WE WORK

We don’t duplicate — we connect. The Puget Sound is home to excellent organizations serving immigrants, students, and workers. What’s been missing is a bridge built by Haitians, for Haitians: someone who speaks Kreyòl, understands the journey, and makes sure our community actually reaches the help that exists. Lakay Toussaint partners with legal service providers, schools, workforce agencies, and community organizations across the region — and we’re always looking for new partners who share our commitment.

PAGE 4 — EVENTS

SA K AP VINI × OUR EVENTS

Intro: > Four times a year, the lakay opens its doors wide. Come for the food, stay for the family.

1804: A Haitian Independence Day Celebration — every New Year

On January 1, 1804, Haiti declared itself the first free Black republic in the world. Every New Year, we gather to honor that legacy the Haitian way — with soup joumou, music, history, and celebration. The first 1804 celebration in Seattle’s history was ours, and it belongs to all of us.

Jou Drapo Ayisyen × Haitian Flag Day — every May 18

In 1803 at Arcahaie, Catherine Flon sewed the blue and red together and the Haitian flag was born. Every May 18, we celebrate the flag and the story behind it — with our young people leading the way, as students do in Haiti.

A Taste of Haiti — every Labor Day

The picnic that started it all. Every Labor Day, the community gathers for a day of Haitian food, live music, family activities, and joy — free and open to everyone. This is where Lakay Toussaint was born, and where we welcome everyone home.

Jounen Resous Kominotè × Community Resource Fair — every November

One afternoon, every resource, all in Kreyòl. Our partner organizations gather under one roof — immigration legal help, healthcare enrollment, housing, schools, job training — so every family gets connected. Free and open to all.

DESIGN PRINCIPLES (hand this list to the developer)

- Hero images show joy — dancing, food, family, the flag — never hardship.
- Kreyòl leads on every section header (“MISYON NOU × OUR MISSION”), following the HCX pattern.
- Primary CTAs are invitations (See what’s happening, Become a member); Donate is persistent but secondary.
- Culture leads the navigation; services are framed as the community caring for itself.
- Short paragraphs, three-beat sections — mobile-first readability.
- Consistent numbers everywhere: 60 families, 50 students, 60 workers, $50,000 fund — matching the Master Planning Workbook (UPDATE the workbook to the consolidated three-program model).
- Compliance lines: immigration disclaimer on Program 1; fiscal sponsorship + tax-deductibility line in the footer.
- The annual rhythm is the brand: January (1804) · May (Flag Day) · September (A Taste of Haiti) · November (Resource Fair) — every season, the community hears from Lakay Toussaint.
